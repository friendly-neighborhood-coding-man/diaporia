Answering is not the same as doing.

Most AI tools are good at answering. Real work is harder.

To get something done for you, a system may need to:

  • understand intent you only partly stated
  • discover the information it is missing
  • decide what actually matters
  • form a plan, and check it
  • act through tools
  • verify the effect those actions had

A single fluent answer covers none of that. DIAPORIA is not another chat interface. It turns what you ask for into structured work that can be inspected and verified before anything is executed, and executed only within explicit limits.

How DIAPORIA works

Five stages, in order. The first three happen before anything outside the system changes. The line in the middle is where DIAPORIA stops to check, and to ask you when it matters.

Before any effect

  1. 1

    Understand

    Find the real task, its constraints and the information that is missing.

  2. 2

    Plan

    Turn intent into an inspectable sequence of work.

  3. 3

    Verify

    Check evidence, assumptions and prepared results before acting.

Checks pass, and you approve where it matters.

Effects, within limits

  1. 4

    Act

    Perform permitted actions through bounded execution paths.

  2. 5

    Confirm

    Check the real effect instead of assuming it succeeded.

Which actions DIAPORIA can take is deliberately limited, and grows only as each kind of action can be checked and confirmed. See current status.

Deliberate by design

DIAPORIA separates reasoning from action.

Producing a plan does not give a model the authority to carry it out. Between the plan and its effect, important operations can require:

Structured actions
Each step is a defined action with stated inputs, not free-form text that happens to run.
Explicit checks
Prepared results are checked before they leave the system.
Your approval
Consequential steps wait for you to say yes.
Bounded execution
Actions run within limits set before they start.
Confirmation afterwards
The outcome is checked, not inferred from the absence of an error.

None of this makes a system infallible. It makes its work visible, and it makes mistakes easier to catch before they matter.

Multi-model by design

The model is a resource. The job comes first.

Different models are good at different things. DIAPORIA is designed so that the task, and the role a model plays within it, decide which model is useful, rather than tying the whole system to a single provider.

Which models and providers are available depends on how DIAPORIA is set up and will change as development continues.

A workflow it is being built for

A recurring job from our own week. It shows the kind of work DIAPORIA is being built to handle, not a feature that runs end to end today.

  1. NowUnderstand the schedule: Mondays, 10:00, with two working times derived from it.
  2. 08:00Retrieve evidence: last week’s issues from the right repositories.
  3. 08:00Review the issues: what changed, what is blocked, what needs a decision.
  4. by 09:30Prepare the briefing.
  5. 09:30Verify the artifact: does every point trace back to an issue it cites?
  6. 09:30Ask before the external effect: writing a file to your Desktop.
  7. 09:30Save the briefing.
  8. 09:30Confirm the file exists and holds what was verified.

Recurring scheduling and retrieval from GitHub are still in progress. The checking, approval and confirmation steps are the parts in development now.

Current status

DIAPORIA is under active development. There is no public release yet. This is where things stand.

Working in development

  • Structured intent and planning, under active implementation.
  • Prepared results can be checked before any external effect.
  • Bounded execution for local files.
  • Effects are confirmed after execution rather than assumed.

In progress

  • Recurring, scheduled workflows.
  • A broader range of external actions.
  • Role-specific reasoning and correction.
  • Deeper interaction and audit flows.

Principles

What DIAPORIA is built to hold to. They are principles, not guarantees.

Reason before acting
Ambiguity is not turned directly into execution.
Evidence over confidence
A fluent answer is not the same as verified knowledge.
Authority is bounded
Planning something does not grant permission to do it.
Verify the effect
Success means the intended result actually occurred.
